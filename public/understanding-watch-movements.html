What keeps a watch moving?

A short introduction to quartz, mechanical and automatic movements, without the jargon.

TIME REFINED

Quartz

A battery powers an electronic circuit regulated by a quartz crystal. Quartz watches generally need little day-to-day attention; battery replacement and servicing should follow the model’s instructions.

Mechanical and automatic

A mechanical movement uses energy stored in a wound spring. An automatic movement is mechanical too, with a rotor that winds the spring as you move. Both have model-specific winding and servicing needs.

Choose what suits you

Movement type is a practical preference, not a shortcut to judging an entire watch. Consider maintenance, convenience and the experience you want. Consult the specifications of the exact model.

The movement is the mechanism inside a watch that measures time and drives its display. Understanding the main categories helps separate meaningful product information from assumptions based on appearance.

Quartz movements

A quartz movement uses a battery and a quartz crystal to regulate timekeeping. The electrical signal causes the crystal to oscillate at a stable frequency, which the circuit converts into regular impulses for the hands or display. Quartz watches are generally valued for accuracy, convenience and relatively low maintenance.

Hand-wound mechanical movements

A hand-wound movement stores energy in a mainspring when the crown is turned. That energy is released through a gear train and regulated by an escapement and balance assembly. Winding becomes a deliberate interaction between wearer and object, but the watch must be wound according to its power reserve and instructions.

Automatic mechanical movements

An automatic movement adds a rotating weight that responds to wrist motion and winds the mainspring. It remains mechanical; “automatic” describes the winding system, not electronic accuracy. Wear patterns, power reserve and service needs influence the experience.

Which movement is best?

There is no universal answer. A quartz movement may suit someone who prioritises accuracy and simplicity. A mechanical movement may appeal to someone interested in traditional mechanisms and a more involved ownership experience. The right choice depends on use, maintenance expectations, budget and personal interest.

What buyers should verify

A responsible product page should identify movement type, relevant calibre information, operating guidance, warranty and service expectations. Water resistance, materials and origin are separate facts and should not be inferred from the movement.
